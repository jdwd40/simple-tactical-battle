# WEGO resolution specification

Status: ruleset `duel-v1` approved with the build plan on 2026-10-12. GAME_DESIGN.md contains the initial tunable numbers. These execution semantics govern implementation.

## Time, orders and commitment

Central configuration contains turn duration, tick duration and all gameplay values. Defaults are 10,000ms / 100ms = 100 ticks; durations must be integral tick multiples. Store absolute simulation ticks for cooldowns, exposures, contact age and missile lifetime. Nothing progresses during planning. A committed resolution may be computed ahead of playback; presentation pause neither changes that result nor runs additional simulation steps.

For each side, capture its observation and planner memory at planning entry. AI planning depends on that snapshot and a dedicated seed/turn/side stream, never on the player's draft. Editing drafts cannot reroll the enemy plan. Validate and freeze both order envelopes before any tick runs. Validation receives that side's observation and public rules/map only. Invalid shape, unknown contact alias, out-of-bounds/blocked/unreachable destination, unavailable equipment or zero missile ammunition are structural errors. A laser awaiting reacquisition/range/cooldown/energy is a conditional intent, not a reason to reveal a hidden ship or reject the whole turn. Cloak plus a scan/shot is legal, with an explicit warning that successful activity breaks cloak until a later planning phase.

The active sensor order requests one pulse on the turn's first tick. Consume 12 energy if available after recharge and higher-priority grants, or report failure and remain passive; mark the pulse attempt spent in either case. Successful tracking lasts 20 ticks and creates 30 ticks of exposure. Sensor and weapon intents reset at planning entry; destination and actual cloak state carry into the draft. Abandon/restart is a separate encounter action, never a playback speed control or a combat draw: leave the encounter without awarding a combat outcome and create fresh session state on restart. Previously committed resolution records remain immutable.

No committed destination, target or policy can change during execution. Weapon intents may wait for eligibility under that committed policy; this is not replanning. Valid orders cannot fail commitment because of an unseen enemy position or hidden enemy order. Previews likewise use only observations and public terrain.

## Geometry and simultaneity

Use integer coordinates with 1,000 units per cell; ships have radius 250 units and missile direct collision radius 100 units. Cell centres are `(column*1000+500, row*1000+500)`. Keep ship circles within the arena. Asteroids are solid cell rectangles.

Route costs are 1,000 cardinal / 1,414 diagonal. Traverse by integer distance progress and integer interpolation, preserving progress without repeated rounding drift. Pathfinding uses octile distance and a stable `(f, h, row, column)` tie order; neighbour enumeration is N, E, S, W, NE, SE, SW, NW. A revised route first connects to its current free cell centre, then follows the new route. Every connection/movement is swept against static obstacles; stop at the last safe point if necessary. Identical destinations retain existing route progress. Hold cancels movement immediately on the next committed turn.

The 100ms tick boundary is the unit of combat simultaneity. All eligible beams and missile impacts assigned to that boundary form one damage batch. Effects on different ticks occur chronologically. Fractional sweep positions identify a missile's first collision and splash geometry; they do not create sub-tick firing initiative. This quantisation deliberately allows a ship lethally hit in a tick to complete a shot eligible for the same batch.

## Tick stages

For interval `(t, t+1]`, stages execute in this order for both sides:

1. **Clocks and recharge.** Expire exposure/scan timers and grant regeneration to ships alive at tick start, capped at maximum energy. Use integer milli-energy units. Cooldown readiness is an absolute tick comparison. Missile expiry remains a stage-6 sweep endpoint, not an early removal that could cancel its last valid impact.
2. **Policy and resource grants.** Allocate each ship's energy in the fixed priority cloak sustain → its single first-tick scan attempt → propulsion → weapon. Successful scanning latches cloak off for this turn; a later successful weapon discharge does likewise. Exposure-suppressed or latched-off cloak costs nothing. Activity after an already-funded sustain payment does not refund it. A failed pulse is spent without cost/exposure and stays passive; failed cloak sustain latches cloak off for that turn. A later turn can explicitly request cloak again, with exposure expiry and funding required. Unfunded propulsion pauses for this tick and may resume later. No resource can become negative.
3. **Movement.** Compute routes and movement from the same prior snapshot, then apply both movements together. Ship overlap has no combat effect. Retain start/end segments for collision sweeps.
4. **Pre-fire sensing.** Compute both sides' contacts from the same moved state, funded sensing modes, terrain and exposure. Fresh detections at this stage can satisfy a laser lock. Losing a lock makes the last measured position stale.
5. **Weapon proposals.** Independently evaluate each unspent intent against that same pre-damage snapshot and its own fresh observations. Check lock/range/line of sight, ammunition, cooldown and remaining energy. Collect successful shots; consume their costs/ammunition, mark the turn intent spent, set cooldowns, add exposure and latch cloak off simultaneously. An unfired intent keeps waiting; at turn end report why it could not complete, without hidden details. Cooldowns persist across turns.
6. **Projectile sweep and damage batch.** Move missiles present at tick start. Sweep against asteroid rectangles and opposing moving ship circles using relative ship/missile segments, not final positions alone. Also test plotted arrival, map exit and expiry; stop at the earliest fraction. An exact tie favours asteroid obstruction, then ship contact, then plotted arrival, then exit/expiry. New missiles start at their moved launcher's position and travel beginning next tick. Build all beam/direct/splash effects from the pre-damage alive set; sample splash positions at the impact fraction. Each missile inflicts its configured damage once per eligible victim, guaranteeing the directly struck victim without adding a second splash hit. Arrival/ship/asteroid impacts detonate; exit/expiry is harmless removal. Sum damage per ship, then apply shields, hull and destruction together. A later enumeration order cannot cancel another eligible shot. Remove only resolved missiles; destroying a launcher does not remove its missiles.
7. **Observation and recording.** Refresh observer knowledge for weapon emissions and visible consequences, filter events, append immutable side-specific frames, and advance the absolute tick. Coalesce pre-fire/post-fire measurements into one knowledge update per tick; estimate velocity from the preceding distinct-tick sighting, never a zero-time difference. Preserve a detection preceding an observed destruction in this batch. A newly revealed firing signature can enable retaliation on the next tick, never a second eligibility pass in this tick. Record own interruptions once per transition rather than flooding the log.

The engine must preserve these stages even if internal implementation combines pure calculations. IDs/event output are canonically ordered for serialization; ordering is never combat initiative. Random draws use explicit independent streams or seed/turn/tick/purpose keys, so unrelated hidden events cannot shift AI randomness.

## Information and replay

Authoritative state contains exact entities, private contact-to-entity mappings, routes, orders, projectiles and internal events. Observations contain own ship, public terrain, measured/stale contacts, observed hostile ordnance, own missile telemetry and permitted events. Opponent internals never enter presentation objects.

Own missile telemetry can report its location/detonation, not unseen target health or an unobserved kill. Hostile missiles are detected within the observer's funded sensor range (7 passive / 10 active cells) and line of sight; their plotted destinations are never disclosed. A lost hostile missile becomes an age-labelled last sighting, not a continuously moving hidden track. Its uncertainty uses the public missile speed.

Ship contact memory retains the last measured position/tick and velocity estimated from sightings. The uncertainty envelope expands at public maximum speed and is clipped to the map, not to secret routes. Losing an unseen ship or missile never deletes memory because of its actual destruction. A dead observer receives no new sensor detections, while previously recorded knowledge remains available.

Store one public frame for every tick plus the starting planning frame. Replay at tick `n` can access only frames/events at or before `n`; final outcome, future tracks and end-of-turn assessment are withheld until their recorded reveal time. Do not interpolate a contact through an interval in which it was unknown. Pause, speed, seek and skip change only the presentation cursor. Returning to planning restores the final observation and clears the replay cursor.

## Destruction and turn boundaries

Finish every committed normal 100-tick turn. A destroyed ship stops future actions from the following tick; all shots eligible for its destruction batch remain valid, and the surviving ship completes its own committed intent. At a completed turn boundary, a destroyed ship plus no live missiles permits final classification: only frigate destroyed = victory; only Shadow-Dancer destroyed = defeat; both destroyed = mutual destruction. Reveal that result only at its recorded assessment timestamp.

If the player survives and missiles remain, return to ordinary planning. The player can manoeuvre to evade them. An observed frigate destruction permits a truthful "ordnance still active" assessment and makes subsequent weapon intents hold fire. When that destruction was unobserved, do not publish a terminal-pending flag, change phase early, disarm the player or remove the stale contact: normal observation-based orders remain available. Any additional blind launches consume the finite ammunition and postpone finalisation until all missiles have impacted/expired. There is no enemy planner call for a destroyed actor. The AI's decision inputs remain its own observation/memory, never the hidden ending flag.

If the player is destroyed and missiles remain, player planning cannot continue, but the living frigate still receives normal WEGO planning opportunities. Automatically advance 100-tick windows: at each boundary its planner receives its own observation, memory and fixed random state, selects legal evasive/resource-aware orders, and commits them without player orders or privileged destruction knowledge. If it observed Shadow-Dancer's destruction, it must hold fire; otherwise its normal legal policies may still launch blind missiles using finite ammunition. This restriction must be derived from the observer's remembered confirmed destruction, equally for either side. Both ships destroyed drains missiles with no new orders/planner calls. Automatic presentation follows observed own destruction; an unseen frigate destruction alone never changes the player's phase.

All missiles expire. With lifetime at most 150 ticks and 100-tick windows, the automatic tail needs at most `ceil(lifetime / turnTicks) * (remaining survivor missile ammunition + 1)` windows; without new launches that is at most two. Each extension requires spending finite ammunition; beams create no persistent ordnance. Check this bound in headless tests and stop/report an internal invariant failure rather than inventing a draw if it is exceeded. Outcome classification still waits for a completed window with no missiles.

There is no turn-limit gameplay draw. Headless diagnostics stop after 200 regular turns, recording a cutoff separately from any outcome. Restart constructs fresh state, streams, memory, drafts and replay; same-seed restart retains scenario/ruleset/seed, while a new seed is generated outside the engine and recorded.
